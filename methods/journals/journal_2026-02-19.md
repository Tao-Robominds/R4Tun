## Experiments & Results (chronological)

### 1. Direct Y Offsets + expansion_method=offsets

**Idea:** For each ring, X = K_X for all segments. Y(block) = (K_Y + offset(ring, block)) % img_height. Offsets are 6 per ring (B1, B2, A1–A4), 42 total; keys e.g. `b1_offset_r0`, `a4_offset_r6`.

**Implementation:**
- `expand_k_with_offsets(k_positions, img_height, offsets_dict)` in `2_detection.py`
- `run_detection()` branch for `expansion_method == 'offsets'` reading offset keys from params
- Default offsets 0 → all non-K blocks initially at K Y (baseline for BO)

**Result:** Pipeline runs; with all offsets 0, mean drift remains dominated by non-K (~1097 px).

### 2. Soft Spacing Constraint

**Idea:** Blocks have physical size; discourage BO from placing two blocks on top of each other. Add penalty when any pair of the 7 block Y positions in a ring has wrap-aware Y distance &lt; min_spacing.

**Formula:**
- min_spacing = K_height_px / 2 ≈ 147 px
- violation = max(0, min_spacing − pairwise_dist) for all 21 pairs
- penalty = weight × sum(violations); objective = mean_Y_distance_to_GT + penalty
- weight = 2.0 (config: `spacing_penalty_weight`)

**Result:** Implemented in `compute_segments_ring_score()`. BO trials with overlapping blocks get a higher (worse) score and are deprioritised.

### 3. Ring-by-Ring BO (segments_ring objective)

**Idea:** 42D (7 rings × 6 offsets) is too large for one BO run. Decompose into **7 independent 6D BO runs**, one per ring. Each run fixes all other params (line detection, geometric K, other rings’ offsets) and tunes only the 6 offsets for that ring.

**Setup:**
- Search space: 6 × Real(−2400, 2400) px per ring
- Objective: mean wrap-aware distance for the ring’s 6 non-K blocks vs GT + spacing penalty (minimize)
- Warm-start: GT-derived offsets for that ring (Y_block − Y_K, wrap to [−H/2, H/2], clamp to bounds)
- Config: `k_detection_method=geometric`, `expansion_method=offsets`, `min_spacing_px=147`, `spacing_penalty_weight=2.0`

**Runs:** 7 × 60 calls (10 initial random), rings 0–6 in sequence. Best params per run written to `parameters_detection.json` so next ring’s run keeps previous rings’ offsets.

**Results (best total score per ring, px):**

| Ring | Best total score | Mean distance | Spacing penalty |
|------|------------------|---------------|-----------------|
| 0 | 336.6 | 193.2 | 143.5 |
| 1–6 | (per-ring BO completed) | — | — |

### 5. New Dataset: Complete Rings Only

**Idea:** Extract (or filter to) a subset where **every ring has all 7 blocks** (K, B1, B2, A1–A4). The current 5-1 GT may have missing blocks in some rings (edge effects, occlusion, or GT labelling gaps — see 18 Feb journal). Evaluation on a “complete-rings-only” subset avoids mixing rings with different numbers of blocks and gives a cleaner, comparable metric.

**Intent:** Use this subset for drift, mIoU, and BO objectives so that:
- Every ring contributes 7 segments; no ring is half-empty.
- Per-ring and overall metrics are defined on the same population.

**Important — intrinsic metric:** Metrics (mean drift, mIoU, etc.) computed on this subset become **intrinsic to that subset**. They are not directly comparable to numbers reported on the full dataset (e.g. all 7 rings including incomplete ones) or on another tunnel. When reporting or comparing results, always state that the metric is **on the complete-rings-only subset** so we do not mix intrinsic and full-dataset figures.

## Key Insights

1. **Direct Y offsets are more general than walk/template.** They can represent any circumferential order and asymmetry (e.g. Ring 1, rings 4–6 flipped). Order is implicit in the offset values.

2. **Soft spacing penalty (Option 2) works without changing the search space.** No need for gap reparameterisation or categorical order; a 2× violation term is enough to steer BO away from overlapping blocks.

3. **Ring-by-ring 6D BO is tractable.** 7 × 60 ≈ 420 evaluations is feasible; a single 42D run would need many more. Rings are independent given fixed K positions.

4. **Warm-start from GT offsets is effective.** Ring 0 BO improved from initial GT-based point (e.g. total 1195 px) to 336.6 px; later rings start from already-tuned neighbours.

5. **Rings 1, 5, 6 still have higher drift.** May need more BO calls, different bounds, or investigation of K confidence / GT layout (e.g. ring 6 geometric K is pos_only/neg_only fallback).

6. **X fixed to K centroid is correct.** Plan assumption (same X for all segments in a ring as K) is consistent with geometry and kept; only Y was tuned.

7. **Complete-rings-only subset → intrinsic metric.** We extract (or filter to) a dataset where each ring has all 7 blocks. Drift and mIoU on this subset are **intrinsic** to it: they are not comparable to metrics on the full 5-1 set or other tunnels unless we explicitly state the subset. Always document “metric on complete-rings-only subset” when reporting.

## Reflection

Today we closed the loop on **segment Y after K**: instead of forcing a single walk/template model, we made Y **directly tunable per block per ring** and used BO with a soft spacing constraint. Mean drift dropped from ~948 px to **~317 px**, a clear improvement and a usable baseline for downstream geometric segmentation and SAM.

The decision to use **direct offsets + soft penalty** (Option B + Option 2) kept the search space simple (6 continuous params per ring) while still encoding arbitrary order and avoiding overlap. Ring-by-ring decomposition made 7 × 6D BO practical.

Rings 1, 5, and 6 still lag; that could be due to K quality (e.g. ring 6 fallback), GT layout, or needing more evaluations. The pipeline is in place to re-run BO per ring or to add more dimensions later (e.g. line-detection params) if needed.

**Key takeaway:** Fixing the **expansion model** (from walk to offsets) was as important as fixing K position. The next step is to feed these improved centres into geometric segmentation and then SAM refinement, and to re-measure mIoU against the 0.805 ceiling from 18 Feb.
