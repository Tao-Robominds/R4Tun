## Step 7 Deploy: Iterative Reflection Proof v3 (tunnel 4/5 push)

After Step 7's single-pass reflection on the held-out panel showed strong gains for tunnels 1/2/3 (mean delta +0.017 / +0.045 / +0.042) but only +0.002 / +0.003 for 4/5, we ran an aggressive iterative search aimed at the user-stated 0.4-0.5 mIoU target for tunnels 4/5.

### Diagnosis of the held-out 4/5 baseline

Held-out 4/5 rings inherit the BO-calibrated `single_ring_visual_slot_template` from the same tunnel (e.g. `4-3/r170` reuses `4-3/r179`'s template). The template encodes absolute `y_frac` positions; held-out unwraps have a different rotational offset, so the template lands at the wrong phase and the visual_layout snap fails. This is the dominant reason tunnel 4/5 baseline mIoU sits at 0.04-0.13 even though BO calibration achieved 0.40-0.72.

### v3 iterative search

Script: `methods/plans/scripts/iterative_reflection_search_v3.py`. Per ring it runs a multi-round intrinsic-only search with three candidate axes:

- 12 cyclic offsets `k/12` of the visual-layout template's `y_frac`s
- 4 fine offsets `±0.015, ±0.030` when `G_layout` is the weakest baseline guardrail
- 6 `ring_offset` perturbations (±50/±100/±200)
- Scalar jitter on `min_score`, `snap_px`, `binary_threshold`, `hough_min_length`, `hough_max_gap`

Held-out mIoU/OA is recorded but never used inside the loop. Acceptance is intrinsic-only via `J_reflect = S_boundary · G_pre · G_layout · G_stability` plus guardrails.

### Results on held-out tunnel 4/5 (n=30)

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| mean mIoU A0 (no reflection) | 0.133 |
| mean mIoU A1 (single-pass) | 0.136 |
| mean mIoU v3 (iterative intrinsic) | **0.158** |
| mean mIoU oracle (mIoU-best in v3 candidate pool) | **0.272** |
| share intrinsic >= 0.4 | 0.10 |
| share oracle >= 0.4 | **0.27** |
| paired t (v3 vs A0), p | 0.24 (not significant) |
| paired t (oracle vs A0), p | **9.9e-5** (significant) |
| 95% bootstrap CI on mean delta v3 vs A0 | [-0.010, +0.069] |
| 95% bootstrap CI on mean delta oracle vs A0 | [+0.084, +0.199] |

### Interpretation

- The rotation candidate axis is the right one: 27% of held-out 4/5 rings have a candidate that exceeds 0.4 mIoU (often 0.5-0.7), with a confidence-bounded mean +0.14 mIoU lift over A0 (p < 1e-4).
- The intrinsic ranker captures only part of that: 10% of rings end above 0.4. We tried two intrinsic-only re-rankers (rotation prior, rotational stability) and neither closed the gap.
- The bottleneck is `J_reflect` itself: among rotation candidates it is sometimes anti-correlated with mIoU, because high-density wrong-phase line detections inflate `S_boundary` without aligning blocks.
- 10/30 rings: v3 helps (mean +0.113); 10/30: unchanged; 10/30: hurt.

The user's 0.4-0.5 target for tunnel 4/5 is therefore **not yet met by intrinsic-only iteration**. The v3 evidence is that the candidate space we built reaches it, but the intrinsic objective needs a structural-template-alignment metric (e.g. labelmap-based label-spread, K-block-shape consistency, per-label fragmentation) to pick the right candidate without GT.

## Step 7 v4: Structural-Alignment intrinsic + cross-ring consensus (tunnel 4/5)

Following the v3 recommendation, we built a labelmap-based structural-alignment metric and wired it into the iterative search. We also tried a cross-ring tunnel-consensus rule on top of the cached v4 candidates.

### What was added

- `bo/structural_alignment_metrics.py` computes six intrinsic, GT-free signals from `detection/labelmap.npy` plus the calibrated detection parameters: `block_count_match`, `k_height_match` (mass-based 90% span vs `k_expected_height_px`), `k_uniqueness` (significant connected components only), `k_centrality` (largest CC column-coverage), `block_height_uniformity`, `per_label_fragmentation`. Combined into `G_structural ∈ [0, 1]`.
- `methods/plans/scripts/iterative_reflection_search_v4.py` runs the same multi-round multi-armed search as v3 but uses `J_reflect_v4 = S_boundary · G_pre · G_layout · G_stability · (0.05 + 0.95 · G_structural)`. Saves per-candidate labelmaps under `logs/iterative_reflection_proof_v4/candidate_labelmaps/<tunnel>/r<ring>/cand_NNNN/`.
- `methods/plans/scripts/iterative_reflection_v4_cross_ring.py` re-ranks v4 candidates per tunnel: pick the rotation `kind` that maximizes the geometric mean of `J_reflect * struct_factor` across all held-out rings of one tunnel section.

### Why the structural metric did not close the gap

Inspecting `4-3/r171`'s candidate cache directly: the **good rotation** (`rot6/12`, mIoU 0.569) has `G_structural = 0.20`, and the **bad winner** (`rot_fine-0.015`, mIoU 0.075) also has `G_structural = 0.20`. They tie on the structural signal, so v4 falls back to picking the bad winner because its raw `J_reflect` is 3.3× larger. Furthermore, several other bad rotations (`rot9/12`, `rot3/12`) have *higher* `G_structural` (0.42–0.55) than the good rotation. `G_structural` measures **internal labelmap consistency**, not **template-to-physical-evidence alignment**, so it cannot break this tie.

### Why cross-ring consensus did not close the gap

Per-ring oracle data shows the optimal rotation differs across rings of the same tunnel: e.g. `4-3/r170` peaks at `rot10/12`, `4-3/r171` at `rot6/12`; `4-7/r300` at `rot_fine-0.015`, `4-7/r301` at `rot_fine-0.030`; `4-8/r330` at `rot_fine-0.015`, `4-8/r334` at `rot7/12`. So the "shared rotation across rings of one tunnel" assumption is only approximate — small per-ring axial variations break it. The aggregator therefore retreats to `rot0/12` (the unmodified template) for 9 of 12 multi-ring tunnels, gaining a small +0.006 nudge.

### Status of the user's tunnel 4/5 target

- **Candidate space**: 27% of held-out 4/5 rings have a candidate above 0.4 mIoU; mean lift +0.14 over A0 (paired-t p = 1.1e-4). This part of the proof is solid.
- **Intrinsic ranker**: 5 different rankers (raw J, rotation prior, rotational stability, G_structural, cross-ring consensus) all cluster at 0.155–0.161 mean mIoU, 10% share above 0.4. None is significant by paired t-test on n = 30.
- The user-stated 0.4–0.5 target for tunnel 4/5 is **reachable but not yet reached intrinsically**. To close the gap further we need a metric that anchors the labelmap to physical depth/curvature evidence at predicted boundaries.

## Step 7 v5: Physical-evidence anchor + minimum-information study

The user redirected: "carry on physical-evidence anchor — that was actually our first instinct (use ontology to align with mIoU). We can also reach a conclusion like with what level of minimum information, we can use intrinsic metrics effectively to align with mIoU/OA". So we ran a focused empirical study to nail down the minimum-information question.

### Study design

For every cached v4 candidate (954 in total across 27 held-out 4/5 rings) we computed 9 intensity-derived intrinsic features by projecting each held-out ring's points-in-class via `pixel_to_point.pkl` and the candidate's labelmap. Calibration anchors come from one BO-calibrated ring per tunnel (from `logs/detection_boundary_structural_panel_v3/templates_gt_calibrated/`), giving us the per-class intensity / r-std vector at design time.

Features tested: `I_high_minus_low` (mean intensity of predicted {K,A4,B2} minus {A1,A2,A3,B1}), `intensity_corr` (Pearson vs calibration), `intensity_spearman` (rank), `class_separation` (std-of-means / mean-of-stds), `per_class_tightness`, `group_purity` (uses ring's own intensity median), `group_purity_high/low`, `rstd_spearman` (vs calibration r-std).

### Headline result

**Pooled vs per-ring correlations diverge sharply for intensity features**, which is the structural diagnosis we needed:

| feature | pooled Spearman | per-ring mean Spearman | per-ring positive rings |
|---|---|---|---|
| `per_class_tightness` (T0) | **+0.384** | -0.044 | 6/27 |
| `class_separation` (T0) | +0.106 | +0.033 | 5/27 |
| `intensity_corr` (T1.5) | -0.032 | -0.106 | 7/27 |
| `intensity_spearman` (T1.5) | -0.015 | -0.113 | 7/27 |
| `I_high_minus_low` (T1.5) | -0.044 | -0.083 | 6/27 |
| `group_purity` (T2) | -0.040 | -0.093 | 5/27 |
| `J_reflect` (T1, baseline) | +0.367 | **+0.183** | 14/27 |

`per_class_tightness` looks attractive pooled (+0.38) but its per-ring average is -0.04. That divergence has a clean structural explanation: cyclic rotations of a labelmap only **relabel** classes; the multiset of per-class intensity statistics is invariant under that relabeling. So intensity-based features capture *between-ring* quality variation but cannot break *within-ring* phase ambiguity.

### What design-time information actually moves the needle

| tier | information used | best within-ring intrinsic Spearman | mIoU intrinsic | mIoU oracle |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | labelmap structure only | +0.07 (G_structural) | 0.156 | 0.273 |
| T1 | BO-calibrated template per tunnel | +0.18 (J_reflect) | 0.158 | 0.273 |
| T1.5 | + per-class intensity profile from one calibration ring per tunnel | -0.11 to +0.03 | 0.158 | 0.273 |
| T2 | + per-ring intensity median (free at deploy time) | -0.10 to -0.05 | 0.158 | 0.273 |

Adding the calibration intensity profile (T1.5) and the held-out ring's own intensity median (T2) **does not improve within-ring intrinsic discrimination on tunnel 4/5**. The bottleneck is rotational phase ambiguity in the unwrap, which no intensity-based feature can resolve.

### What would actually break the ambiguity

To go beyond the current 10% of held-out 4/5 rings reaching >= 0.4 mIoU, we need a feature that is **not invariant under cyclic relabeling** — i.e., one that is anchored to a physical reference. Three options, in order of cost:

1. **Gravity-aligned unwrap (preprocessing change, lowest cost)** — re-do the unwrap so "bottom of tunnel" is always at the same y. This removes the rotation degree of freedom entirely and our existing `J_reflect` ranker already picks correctly when the rotation axis is collapsed.
2. **Depth-discontinuity anchor (metric change)** — score predicted K-block boundary against the measured depth-discontinuity strength at that y. Currently our `depth_map_outlier.npy` has 0.09% valid-pixel density, too sparse for this signal to be reliable; fixing density via a denser unwrap would unlock it.
3. **Cross-ring physical consistency** — already tried in v4.5; gives only +0.006 because per-ring phase still drifts within a tunnel section.

### Conclusion of the minimum-information study

Within the **purely intrinsic, no-spatial-anchor** information regime, our current intrinsic stack (T1) at +0.18 per-ring Spearman is the *practical ceiling*. The candidate space holds an oracle gain of +0.14 mean mIoU on held-out 4/5 (paired-t p = 1.1e-4), so 80% of the available headroom is locked behind the rotational ambiguity. Closing it requires either a preprocessing change (gravity-aligned unwrap) or a denser depth signal — neither is a metric tuning problem.

## 2026-05-05 — Gravity unwrap + depth-disc anchor (Step 7 v5)

User asked to close the minimum-information story by trying both the
preprocessing-change (gravity-aligned unwrap) and the metric-change
(depth-discontinuity anchor) directly.

### Key findings

1. **Audit correction**: `depth_map_outlier.npy` is 0.09% dense but the
   regular `depth_map.npy` is 70% dense. The depth-disc metric was never
   actually blocked.
2. **Diagnosis confirmed**: the current unfolder `_ring_unfolding.unfold_
   single_ring` anchors `theta=0` to a per-ring PCA axis + RANSAC
   ellipse centre. Physical-bottom `theta_frac` varies from 0.090
   (r171) to 0.545 (r179) to 0.924 (r170) across a single tunnel —
   the rotational DOF the iterative-reflection search was trying to
   resolve is created by the unwrap itself.
3. **Gravity unwrap implementation** (`methods/plans/scripts/gravity_
   align_unwrap.py`): bin `z` by `theta`, locate the min-`z` bin, shift
   `theta' = (theta − theta_bottom) mod range`, then cross-correlate the
   shifted `z(theta)` profile against a per-tunnel reference profile to
   disambiguate direction. Implemented as a cheap row-shift of
   `depth_map.npy` + `pixel_to_point.pkl`, preserving 70% density.
4. **Evaluation metric correction**: the existing naive mIoU compares
   ring-specific GT `segment` IDs to canonical pred class IDs (`K=1,
   B1=2, …`). These are not the same labelling scheme, so naive mIoU
   rewards whoever is lucky with label ordering. Switched the
   comparison to permutation-invariant mIoU (Hungarian match over the
   class×class IoU matrix) for a fair cross-ring quality measure.
5. **Gravity results on held-out tunnel 4/5** (12 rings, perm-mIoU):
   A0 mean 0.309 → Gravity mean 0.327 (Δ +0.018). Hits ≥ 0.40 grow
   5/12 → 6/12. Big wins: `4-3/r170` (+0.099), `5-7/r322` (+0.088),
   `4-3/r171` (+0.032). Rings with broken source preprocessing
   (`5-1/r110`, `5-1/r111` have 1×1 `depth_map.npy`) or genuine detection
   failures (`4-4/r212`, `4-4/r217`) are unchanged, which is the
   correct behaviour.
6. **Depth-disc anchor negative result**: block boundaries in tunnels
   4/5 produce tiny depth gradients (adjacent blocks are radially
   co-planar at `r ≈ 3.75 m`). Anchor scores are 0.006–0.121 across
   all rings. The *dominant* depth discontinuities are at the top-joint
   notch (`r → 2.0 m`) and the floor-reflection band — neither of which
   is a block boundary. Use as a boundary-evidence score is
   fundamentally miscalibrated to this geometry.
7. **Minimum-information story closed**: the lowest-cost architectural
   change that breaks the rotational-phase bottleneck is the
   preprocessing change (gravity-aligned unwrap). Depth-discontinuity
   probing would need to be refactored as a *landmark*-locator rather
   than a boundary scorer, and that is largely redundant with gravity
   alignment.
