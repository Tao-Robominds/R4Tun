# Guidelines for Responding to Reviewers

Distilled from four prior response letters in `methods/reviews/`:
`Comments.docx`, `JRNGTENG-SS-22-00899_Response_to_reviewers_final_bbs.docx`,
`Response to Reviewer Comments PH bbs.docx`, `Responses to Reviewers_R1 bbs.docx`.

## 1. Document Skeleton

```
<Paper Title>
<Authors>
<Date / Article number>

<Opening paragraph: thank editor + reviewers, note manuscript revisions
 are highlighted in yellow, point-by-point responses below.>

(Optional) Associate Editor / Editor
    Comment: ...
    Response: ...

Reviewer 1
    Reviewer's comment: 1. ...
    Response: ...

    Reviewer's comment: 2. ...
    Response: ...

Reviewer 2
    ...

(Optional) Annotated PDF / Minor edits
    Lxx ...
    Response: ...

References  (only the new ones added because of revisions)
```

Always:
- Number reviewer comments in order (1, 2, 3 ...).
- Repeat the reviewer's comment verbatim (or a faithful summary) before the response — never reply without quoting the comment.
- Close with a one-line thank-you only when natural; do not add boilerplate at the end of every comment.

## 2. Opening Paragraph (template)

> We are grateful to the AE and reviewers for their time in reviewing the manuscript and for their useful and insightful feedback. We have responded to the queries individually below and modified the manuscript as appropriate. Changes to the manuscript are highlighted in yellow.

Variants used in the corpus add: word-limit acknowledgement, deadline note, or "we have endeavoured to address all concerns while minimising the impact on word count."

## 3. Tone

- **Polite, professional, grateful, never defensive.** Open with phrases such as
  *"We thank the reviewer for ..."*, *"This is a sensible suggestion."*,
  *"Excellent point."*, *"The reviewer raises an important point."*
- **Concede readily when correct.** *"Correct — thank you for spotting this typo."*
- **Disagree respectfully and with evidence.** Cite the manuscript, other
  reviewers, or the literature; never argue from authority alone.
  Example from the corpus when one reviewer called the writing "messy":
  *"The authors are surprised by this comment given that [the authors] are
  native English speakers ... Reviewer 1 described the manuscript as 'high
  quality' and 'clearly presented'. Reviewer 3 described the work as 'well
  documented'. The authors welcome the reviewer to highlight specific [issues]."*
- **No emojis, no exclamation marks, no informal phrasing.**
- **Keep responses short.** A single sentence is fine for trivial fixes;
  longer responses only when justification requires it.

## 4. Per-Comment Response Patterns

Pick the pattern that fits the comment. Each pattern has worked verbatim in the corpus.

### 4.1 Trivial typo / wording fix
> Response: The associated text has been updated.
>
> Response: Corrected.
>
> Response: Correct — thank you for spotting this typo. Z has been replaced with I to indicate the second moment of cross-sectional area.

### 4.2 Suggestion accepted with content change — point to where
> Response: This is a sensible suggestion. The section "Real-time monitoring platform" has been extended to include the additional information recommended.
>
> Response: We have added additional sentences to clarify these points (see lines 194–196 and 206–207).
>
> Response: That figure (now Figure 14 in the revised version) has been updated to include a longer time history of these movements.

Always tell the reviewer **where** the change appears: section name,
line numbers in the revised manuscript, or new figure / table number.

### 4.3 Suggestion accepted — quote the new text
When the change is a discrete sentence/paragraph, paste it inside the response in quotes with the page or line:
> Response: Thank you. We have added the following to the text:
>
> "Pile driveability predictions in this study were performed using industry-standard 1D wave equation analyses. Tsetas et al. (2021) compared 1D models with a 3D model that accounted for wave dispersion and non-local soil reaction and showed that the 1D models become less accurate for diameters greater than ≈4 m, above the maximum D considered in this study." (pg 5)

### 4.4 Suggestion partially accepted (scope/word-limit constraint)
> Response: Owing to the current length of the manuscript and the word limit for this journal, details of the PLAXIS model have not been included. However, the results of the site investigation have been added.
>
> Response: The authors plan to include the results of more advanced laboratory testing in a follow-up paper based on the development of a numerical model in PLAXIS 2D/3D.

### 4.5 Suggestion declined — justify with evidence
Acknowledge the reviewer's point, then explain the reason and back it with data, references, or a clarifying experiment. Never simply refuse.
> Response: We acknowledge the importance of considering ground movements during pile installation, especially in close proximity to existing structures. However, we are pleased to report that no damage occurred to Savile House during installation. This can be attributed to the stiff Oxford Clay and the implementation of best practices during piling. In response to the reviewer's suggestion, we have included additional data on ground movements (averaged from SH-ML1, SH-ML2 and SH-ML3) during installation of the secant piles abutting Savile House (see new Figure 14(a)).

Pattern: **acknowledge → reason → still-do-something to address it.**

### 4.6 Defending the contribution
If the reviewer questions novelty/significance, respond with concrete
evidence: counts of similar case studies, citations of recent
*Géotechnique* papers with comparable structure, specific data the paper
contributes that is otherwise missing in the literature.
> Response: ... 16 of the 296 case studies reported in Long (2001) related to a combi-wall design, and only one involved a tube and sheet pile combination. ... The temperature-induced prop load data collected during this project provide a valuable contribution to the databases in Twine & Roscoe (1997) and Gaba et al. (2017).

### 4.7 New figure, table or section added
Name the new artifact explicitly:
> Response: We have added a new Fig. 2 to introduce the flow chart of the proposed methodology.
>
> Response: A "Structural Design" section discussing the curved multi-level capping beam and the "arching" effect has now been added.

### 4.8 New references added
List them at the end of the response or in a final References block:
> Response: Discussion of stiff London Clay has been added to the introduction. Additional references have also been added:
> - Gasparre, A., Nishimura, S., Coop, M.R. and Jardine, R.J., 2011. ...
> - Hight, D.W., Gasparre, A., ... 2011. ...
> - Carder, D.R., 1995. ...

## 5. Cross-Referencing the Manuscript

Required forms (use any consistently within one letter):
- `(see lines 194–196)`
- `(pg 7)` or `(pg 17, conclusions)`
- `(see new Fig. 14(a))`, `(now Figure 14)`
- `(see "Application of Observational Method" section)`

When a figure is renumbered, write *"that figure (now Figure X in the
revised version)"* so the reviewer can locate it.

## 6. Quoting Comments and Edits

- Use straight `"quotation marks"` for inserted manuscript text.
- Use the heading `Reviewer's comment:` (or `Comment N:`) and `Response:` (or `Reply:`) consistently. Pick one pair and keep it across the whole letter.
- For batches of minor edits (typos, equation references), group them
  under a heading such as `Annotated PDF` or `Minor edits` with one
  short response per item — `Response: Corrected.`

## 7. Things to Always Do

1. Quote / restate every comment before responding.
2. Tell the reviewer **what** you changed and **where** (section, lines, figure).
3. When inserting new manuscript text, paste it in quotes with the page reference.
4. Highlight all manuscript changes in yellow (mention this once in the opening).
5. Keep responses concise; one sentence is enough for trivial items.
6. Address every single comment — never silently skip one.
7. End with new references introduced by the revision (if any).

## 8. Things to Avoid

1. Do not ignore comments or merge multiple comments into one vague reply.
2. Do not respond with bare *"Done."* — say what was done and where.
3. Do not argue without evidence; never be sarcastic or dismissive.
4. Do not introduce new content in the response that is not also in the revised manuscript.
5. Do not change the numbering scheme mid-letter.
6. Do not add emojis, exclamation marks, or marketing language.

## 9. Length Heuristic

| Comment type                           | Response length             |
|----------------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Typo / single-word fix                 | ≤ 1 sentence                |
| Clarification / wording change         | 1–2 sentences + line refs   |
| New paragraph / figure added           | 2–4 sentences + quoted text |
| Methodological objection               | 1 short paragraph + evidence|
| Challenge to novelty / significance    | 1 paragraph with citations  |
